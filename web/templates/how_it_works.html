How the monster learns

The monster is a reinforcement learning agent. Nobody tells it which room is the right one: it plays thousands of nights against a simulated guard, gets a reward when it reaches the office and a penalty when the night ends, and slowly learns a policy that maximizes its total reward.

3. What the monster can do

Six discrete actions: the index of the room it wants to go to (0–4), or 5 to try to enter the office. Choosing its own room means staying still.

Action masking. Before every decision, moves to rooms that are not connected are disabled, and the office can only be tried from the two corridors. The network never wastes experience on impossible moves, so training is faster.

Terminal rewards define the goal. Shaping rewards are small hints that help early exploration; if training stalls, we remove terms instead of adding more.

5. "It learns where you don't look"

The trained network is frozen inside the game: it does not keep learning while you play. What makes it feel adaptive is what it observes. It knows how long ago each camera was checked and whether you are watching it right now, and during training it learned that rooms nobody checks are the safest way in. Against you, it uses that rule with your habits.
