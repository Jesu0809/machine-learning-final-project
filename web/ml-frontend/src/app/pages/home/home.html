Problem and justification

The problem and its context

The enemies of most games are written by hand as finite-state machines, behaviour trees or planners (Millington, 2019; Orkin, 2006; Isla, 2005). Their rules are fixed, so players learn the pattern in a few tries and the enemy stops being a threat; and every new room or rule needs more hand-written cases.

Who is affected: players of horror and stealth games, whose tension depends on an enemy they cannot predict, and small teams like ours, who cannot afford to hand-tune behaviour for every situation.

Evidence: in our own simulation the hand-written hunter, with the same senses as the network, catches the expert survivor in 91.5% of the nights; and every playtest found situations the rules did not cover (walking past a noise next door, camping a door). Consequences: a predictable enemy, a flat experience and growing development cost.

Justification and contribution

Reinforcement learning lets an agent improve from rewards instead of instructions (Sutton & Barto, 2018), and learned agents have shown strategies their designers did not program, from hide-and-seek (Baker et al., 2020) to complex video games (Berner et al., 2019; Vinyals et al., 2019). Unity ML-Agents (Juliani et al., 2018) brings it into a game engine, as the course requires.

How the intelligent system helps: Pockets’ trained network decides every move from what he hears and sees, catches the expert survivor in 97.8% of the nights (+6.3 points over the hand-written hunter) and never cheats. The playtest studio closes the loop: it records real nights and uses the same network to tell learned decisions from bugs when a player reports strange behaviour.

Objectives

General objective

Develop a first-person horror game whose enemy is a neural network trained with reinforcement learning (PPO in Unity ML-Agents) that hunts the player using only what it perceives, and integrate that model into a web information system where playtests are managed and the model supports the decision of whether a reported behaviour is learned or a bug.

The game

You are the night guard of Bellwether's Winter Circus. Each night you power three sockets with cells (one at a time), complete the night's other tasks and pull the lever of the exit. Pockets, the circus's clown, hunts you through 13 rooms: you can hide in lockers, cabinets and under tables, close doors behind you (he breaks them down) and crawl through the vents.

The seven nights are the same for every player. What changes with skill is how dangerous he is: a threat level per player sets his pace, speed and sight, never what he knows. After the story come the encore (rounds until caught) and the show of the day (the same night for everyone, from the date).

Keyboard and mouse or an Xbox controller, on the desktop or in the browser.
