Reinforcement Learning: Concepts

How an NPC can learn to hunt without anyone writing its rules: an agent, an environment, a reward, and PPO to turn experience into a better policy.

🐻

Agent

Freddy's brain, which picks the next move

🍕

Environment

The pizzeria, its hiding spots and the survivor

🎯

Reward

+1 for a catch, −1 if the survivor escapes

🧭

Policy

The neural network: situation → probability of each action

The agent–environment loop

Every 3.5 seconds of game time, the same cycle:

  1. Observe: Freddy's brain receives 64 numbers: the noises it heard and from which room, where it last saw the player, how long since it checked each room, whether its sense is ready…
  2. Decide: the network outputs a probability for each of the 15 actions and one is chosen.
  3. Act: his 3D body walks to the room, searches the hiding spot or uses its sense.
  4. Reward: during training, the result is scored (+1 catch, −1 escape, small penalties for wasted searches).

Markov Decision Process

StatesWhat Freddy knows (64 values)
Actions15: move to one of 11 rooms or stay, search spot 0–2, sense
TransitionsThe rules of the hunt (hearing, sight, hiding)
RewardCatch, escape, time, wasted searches
Discount γ0.99

Partial observability

Freddy never observes where the player is. He gets noises (sometimes placed in the wrong room) and occasional sightings, so the network has to remember and infer, like a real hunter.

That is also why a thrown party horn fools him: to the network, a noise is a noise.

PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)

The default trainer of Unity ML-Agents. It collects experience with the current policy, estimates how much better or worse each action was than expected (the advantage), and nudges the network toward the good actions, but never too far in one update (a clipped objective, ε = 0.2). That makes training stable with little tuning.

Schulman, J., Wolski, F., Dhariwal, P., Radford, A., & Klimov, O. (2017). Proximal policy optimization algorithms. arXiv:1707.06347.

Action masking

Impossible actions (rooms not connected, spots that do not exist, sense while recharging) are switched off before every decision.

Reward shaping

A small potential-based bonus for getting closer speeds up learning without changing the best policy (Ng et al., 1999).

Domain randomization

Every training night the simulated survivor gets a different personality, so Freddy cannot overfit one play style.

Training vs inference

The network learns offline. In the game it is frozen (an .onnx model): it reacts to you, it does not keep learning.
